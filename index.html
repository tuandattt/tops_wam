Abstract

World action models (WAMs) have shown promising generalization and data efficiency in robot manipulation by coupling action generation with predictions of future visual dynamics. Translating these capabilities into reliable task execution, however, remains a challenge for existing post-training methods. Offline supervised fine-tuning provides dense supervision on expert trajectories, but its flow-matching action expert is trained only on noised demonstration actions and never on the intermediate states produced by its own sampler; task-specific adaptation can also compromise pretrained capabilities. On-policy reinforcement learning trains on the policy’s own behavior, yet sparse task rewards make exploration and credit assignment difficult, while dense reward shaping still provides scalar evaluations rather than direct action targets.

We introduce HINT-WAM, the first on-policy self-distillation framework that supervises the action expert on its own sampling trajectories using hindsight, the future video of the demonstration, as privileged information available to the WAM itself. For each demonstration observation, the student generates its action through its own denoising trajectory, and at the states along this trajectory the same model acts as a teacher conditioned additionally on this hindsight, providing a conditional action distribution as a dense training target, while the student sees only deployment-time inputs and needs no environmental rewards or simulator interaction. To transfer this supervision to a flow-matching action expert, HINT-WAM distills the teacher into the student with an energy score, a distribution-level objective that matches the teacher’s action distribution from the student’s own samples rather than regressing its velocity field.

Method

The teacher and the student are the same network. The teacher additionally reads hindsight: the future video of the demonstration being imitated. The student sees only deployment-time inputs and is trained on the states its own sampler visits.

Stage 0

SFT initialization

Fine-tune the action expert of Fast-WAM on demonstrations with flow matching. The video expert stays frozen in every stage.

Stage A

Hindsight teacher

With the SFT model frozen, a lightweight encoder pools the demonstration's future latent frames into hindsight tokens that the action expert reads through zero-initialized, gated cross-attention adapters (~13M params).

Stage B

On-policy self-distillation

The student rolls out its own ODE, so supervision lands on states it really visits. No simulator and no reward are needed, and nothing is back-propagated through the rollout. “On-policy” refers to the generation of actions: observations come from demonstrations, and the student generates the denoising trajectory of its own action chunk.

Why the energy score?

The student cannot see the future, so from its point of view the teacher's behavior is multi-modal. Regressing the teacher's velocity drives the student toward the teacher's conditional mean, which can be correct under none of the modes. Instead we match distributions with a strictly proper scoring rule that needs only one teacher sample and no action likelihood:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ES}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\bar w_k\Big[\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{m}\|\hat{\mathbf a}_{m,k}-\mathbf a_T\|_2-\tfrac{1}{2M^2}\sum_{m,m'}\|\hat{\mathbf a}_{m,k}-\hat{\mathbf a}_{m',k}\|_2\Big]$$

The first term pulls the student's samples toward the teacher's sample; the second pushes them apart and prevents collapse. Here $\hat{\mathbf a}=\mathbf a_\sigma-\sigma\,v_\theta(\mathbf a_\sigma,\sigma,o)$ is the one-pass implied action. The full objective adds a behavior-cloning anchor: $\mathcal{L}_B=\lambda_{\mathrm{dist}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dist}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{BC}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}$.

Ablations and cost

The off-policy variant replaces the student’s sampler states with the demonstrated action noised to the same points, i.e. the states SFT sees, keeping everything else unchanged. The hindsight teacher helps even on demonstration states, but most of the gain requires supervising the student on the states it reaches at inference.
